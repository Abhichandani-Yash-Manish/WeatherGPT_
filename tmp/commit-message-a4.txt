A4: measure every language whose writing can be checked, and claim none that cannot

The adherence table behind the answer-language gate held two entries, Hindi and Gujarati: an answer promised in
Tamil, Bengali, Punjabi, Odia, Kannada, Telugu, Malayalam, Marathi or Urdu was never checked at all.

- dialogue.SCRIPTS is derived from the language registry now, so all 22 measurable languages are checked and a
  language added there is measured instead of passing silently. English and romanised Hinglish share the Latin
  script and are reported as unverifiable rather than passing by default.
- a romanised request (hi-Latn) used to come back as "not_applicable" while the reader received English. It is
  reported as unverifiable_script with a note that the answer stays in its source language.
- measured live, ten languages, one weather question each with an explicit output language
  (research/reviews/chat-overhaul-20260916/languages.json): hi, gu, ta, mr, pa, od and ur rendered with every
  value as the original characters (6.5-9.1 s); bn and ml were refused as values_did_not_survive and kept the
  source-language answer with the refusal recorded - the service dropped protected placeholders on the written
  sentence, not a gap in the patterns; hi-Latn was answered and never claimed.

measurable language, a Tamil rendering with its values, the unverifiable romanised case and the two honest
failures. 1220 Python tests, 10 component suites (109 printed checks), 27 verification steps, none failing.
