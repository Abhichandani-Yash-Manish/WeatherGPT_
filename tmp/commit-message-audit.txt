Final integration audit: what is wired, what a reader can touch, and how each PS feature gets closed

docs/89 is the review and the closure plan, built from a script that measures rather than narrates
(research/reviews/final-audit-20260917/architecture-inventory.json): 78 engine modules with none orphaned, 63
routes (27 product views, 16 gated GET routes, 20 mutations), 14 capability tools over 12 kinds, 70 registered
sources with 29 connected and wired to chat and 15 blocked, 19 surfaces identical in the vanilla rail and the
React registry, 90 documents, 18 registries, 1287 Python tests plus 10 Node suites and 13 frontend checks.

Two measurements were wrong on the first pass and both corrections are recorded, because that is the habit this
repository runs on: the source ledger keeps connected/wired_to_chat nested under connector (a first pass read
zero connected sources), and route coverage by URL string understates reality here because the suite exercises
handlers directly, so no coverage percentage is published from it.

Two integration gaps were closed in this batch rather than scheduled:
- tests/test_route_inventory.py (5 checks): the POST route table moved to module scope so it can be enumerated at
  all, and the inventory is pinned - callable handlers, no duplicate paths, every product path served, and a
  gated GET list that stays small and under /api/.
- scripts/audit_surface_registry.py (8 checks, now a step of verify_all): the two frontends must agree on the 19
  surfaces, every surface must name the route it reads, every declared route must be served, every product route
  must have a surface or an exemption carrying a reason, and no read surface may be wired to a mutation route.

docs/89 also gives every PS feature the acceptance criteria that would let it be called done, its current state
and the exact remaining delta, plus the ordered closure sequence: finish R1 (port the 110 checks, browser
acceptance), the feature-4 live device journey, feature-5 contradiction handling, per-product coverage
statements, native-speaker language review, voice acceptance, the dated district crosswalk, then the landing page
and owner gate.
